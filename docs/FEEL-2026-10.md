# Motion feel and clarity, October 2026: Hyprune vs Hypr3D

The owner's question, comparing the old Hypr3D plugin with Hyprune: Hypr3D runs at about
30 fps yet feels like 60, and its windows look sharp; Hyprune feels heavier. This note
reads Hypr3D's source (`~/workspace/rick-the-alien/hypr3d`, 20e1baa) instead of guessing,
measures both behaviours in the nested 4K Intel setup, and records what changed.

All measurements are nested (`HYPRUNE_NESTED_4K=1`, Mesa Intel PTL, Lumen Reach 0.9),
`dev/nested/run.sh --feel` (see "Method"). The owner's live session was not used.

## What Hypr3D does (from its source)

- **One update per presented frame, with that frame's real time.** `onRenderStage` (the
  render-stage hook, `src/main.cpp`) computes `dt` from the previous rendered frame and runs
  `update3D(dt)`: physics (Jolt, `dt` clamped to 1/240..1/30 s), movement and mouse look
  (accumulated in the pointer hook, consumed with `1 - exp(-dt / 0.03)`). An 8 ms timer only
  damages the monitor; Hyprland's repaints pace everything. So every presented frame shows
  the camera exactly where it should be at that moment, at 30 fps or 60.
- **Everything at native resolution.** The scene framebuffer is `currentFB->m_size` (full 4K);
  windows are textured quads in the same pass, bilinear (`GL_LINEAR`, no mips). There is no
  dynamic resolution or upscaler; simple shading keeps 4K near 30 fps (the owner's
  screenshot: FPS 32, 118,750 triangles).
- **Same inertia constants as ours** (look 0.03 s, move 0.05 s; Hyprune inherited them),
  but a wider vertical FOV (65 deg vs our 60), faster walking (4.0 m/s, sprint x2.5 vs our
  3.5 m/s, run x1.8) and a small view bob (3.5 cm, 0.008 rad roll). Same look sensitivity.

## What Hyprune did

- A 16 ms `CEventLoopTimer` ran the tick (input, fixed 60 Hz simulation via an accumulator,
  mouse look once per tick), independent of presentation; rendering sampled whatever the
  last tick left. The timer (62.5 Hz) beats against vblank (60 Hz): a shown frame advanced
  by 0, 1 or 2 steps.
- When the GPU was behind (two frames in flight), the renderer repeated the previous frame
  at that moment: irregular new/repeat sequences.
- Room windows are already drawn at native resolution after the upscale (same bilinear
  sampling as Hypr3D), but **workspace walls were composed into a fixed 1280x720 texture**:
  a 16x9 m wall seen from 8 m spans ~3740 of 3840 pixels, a 2.9x magnification (4.7x at
  5 m), and is minified 5x across a court with no mipmaps.

## Method

`dev/nested/feel.py` (`--feel`) walks (W held 3 s) and turns (a steady 500 Hz relative
pointer stream, `nested-pointer stream`) per graphics configuration and reads the renderer's
per-presentation log (new frame, repeated frame, idle re-present; time and camera pose):

- **cadence CV**: coefficient of variation of the spacing between new frames (0 = every
  frame arrives on the same interval);
- **motion CV**: coefficient of variation of the velocity between consecutive new frames
  (0 = every frame advances the same amount per unit time);
- **latency**: key press or first mouse event to the first newly rendered frame that shows
  the motion (render start, same steady clock; 10 trials each). Photons add the GPU frame
  and scanout.

Wall clarity (`HYPRUNE_FEEL_WALL=1`): a terminal of dense text on the Sun Court home wall at
8 m (where the wall spans one 4K screen, text at 0.994x native size) against the same
workspace focused in stock 2D; sharpness = mean edge magnitude of the wall text relative to
the native text resampled (Lanczos) to the same size (1.0 = as sharp as native).

Reading it:

- **Turning was uneven in every configuration (motion CV ~0.18)**, even with no repeated
  frames: mouse look was applied once per 16 ms tick, which beats against the 60 Hz
  presentation. With the tick driven by presentation and the frame's real dt, look and walk
  advance evenly per frame (0.01-0.02).
- **GPU-bound, frames arrived at irregular intervals (cadence CV 0.34)**: new frames and
  repeats mixed at random (16.7 or 33.3 ms gaps). That is the "adventure game on weak
  hardware" feeling. After: a steady 30 fps (CV 0.002) where the frame interval is constant
  and each frame advances by exactly its time, which is what Hypr3D does and why its 30 fps
  feels like more.
- `target_fps` used to steer only dynamic resolution (target 30 still rendered ~58
  irregular frames/s); it now also paces: 30 at 60 Hz is every second presentation, evenly,
  and dynamic resolution spends the budget on a higher scale (0.4375-0.5 vs 0.375).
- Latency: at the default (60 fps) unchanged (8-10 ms input to frame). Running at a steady 30
  costs one frame interval (22-29 ms). Native 4K Low on this iGPU now settles at a steady 30
  instead of an irregular ~58: with the tick following presentation, a frame that misses one
  vblank lands on the next, and at 4K native this iGPU is right at the 16.7 ms boundary. With
  dynamic resolution (the default) it holds 57-60.

## Changes

1. **Walls at native resolution** (`Compose workspace walls at native resolution with
   mipmaps`): a wall with windows is composed at its monitor's pixel size with a full mip
   chain (regenerated only when the composite changes; idle walls cost nothing), sampled
   trilinear + anisotropic in the native pass. A wall without windows keeps the 1280x720
   placeholder. Memory: ~44 MB per wall with windows at 4K.
2. **Steady pacing for explicit targets** (`Pace new frames on a steady divisor of the
   presentation rate`): `target_fps` below the display rate renders new frames on every
   n-th presentation (n = round(refresh / target)); intentional repeats are not counted as
   queue pressure. Automatic slowdowns (on queue depth or GPU timers) were tried and
   rejected: queue depth at render time reflects scheduling phase as much as overload, and a
   slower cadence lets the Intel GPU clock down (8.6 -> 13.8 ms per frame), so the controller
   never stepped back up.
3. **Tick on presentation, move by real time** (`Tick on frame presentation and move by the
   frame's real time`): a tick is queued (doLater, outside the render pass) on each
   `presented` event of the active monitor; the 16 ms timer only drives ticks while nothing
   is presented. Movement and look use the measured dt (capped at 4/60 s; the walker and
   flight already sub-step at 2 ms); recording and replay keep exact fixed steps.
   Render-time interpolation between fixed steps was tried first and made motion worse
   (walk motion CV 0.018 -> 0.20): the timer-driven tick often had not yet produced the state
   for "render time minus one step", so the view stalled.

## Not changed (owner's call)

- **Default target on Intel/4K.** A steady 30 with a higher render scale is closer to the
  Hypr3D experience (sharper world, even motion) at one frame more latency; the default stays
  60 with dynamic resolution.
- **Feel tuning that differs from Hypr3D**: vertical FOV 65 vs our 60, walk 4.0 m/s and
  sprint x2.5 vs our 3.5 m/s and x1.8, a 3.5 cm walk bob. Wider FOV and faster movement make
  the same frame rate feel faster.
- **World shading cost.** Hypr3D's simple shading renders native 4K at ~32 fps; ours spends
  more per pixel (lightmaps, AO, FSR) and runs at 0.375-0.5 scale to hold 60.

## Regression checks

On this branch: CTest 25/25; nested `--climbing` 49, `--m3b` 158, `--autodrive` 29,
`--workspace-homes` 35, `--static-world` 15 checks. The first full pass had one
`--m3b` "area already bound" at bind time and one `--static-world` "turn is animated"
miss; neither came back in reruns (m3b 2x, static-world 3x), and the static-world check is
already fragile on main:
the flip trace polls diagnostics so slowly that it catches at most one mid-turn sample
(main's committed evidence shows the same `[-0.68, -1.0, ...]`).
